Jerbventory Privacy Policy

Effective date: August 10, 2026

Last updated: October 6, 2026

Applies to: Jerbventory for iPhone/iPad and Android

The short version

Jerbventory does not collect your personal data. There are no accounts and no analytics. Your inventory, sales, shows, photos, and financial records stay on your device unless you deliberately export them somewhere.

The one thing the app does talk to is our own pricing service, so that card prices can be refreshed each day. It receives an anonymous device token and the catalog identifiers of cards you track or look at in a catalog, never your records themselves. In versions that sign up for pricing automatically, Apple or Google first confirms to our service that it is the genuine app; we check that confirmation and do not keep it. To stop abuse of that sign-up, the service briefly uses a scrambled form of your network address, which it never stores and forgets within 24 hours. The details are in the section "Our pricing service" below.

What we collect

Jerbventory has no user accounts, no sign-in, no telemetry, no advertising identifiers, no crash reporting, and no analytics SDK of any kind. We do not build a profile of you.

The only data that reaches a server we operate is what the pricing service needs to work, described in "Our pricing service" below: an anonymous device token, the catalog identifiers of cards you track or look at in a catalog, and the ordinary connection records any web server keeps. In versions that offer automatic sign-up, it also receives a one-time confirmation from Apple or Google when the app signs up, and a short-lived scrambled form of your network address used only to limit sign-up attempts.

What stays on your device

All of the following is stored locally, on your device only:

  • Inventory and Collection records, including card details, quantities, condition, and grading
  • Acquisition cost, asking price, market values, and every calculated financial figure
  • Sales, bundles, trades, purchases, fees, expenses, and show records
  • Transaction history, reversals, and closeout results
  • Photos you attach to a Quick Capture or a card
  • App preferences and settings

On Android this is a local database in the app's private storage. On iOS it is a local database in the app's container. Neither is transmitted to us.

Camera, photos, and label scanning

Jerbventory uses the camera only for local actions you choose: attaching a photo to a transaction or card on iOS, and scanning a Jerbventory QR inventory label on Android or iOS.

  • Attached photos are stored on your device only and are never uploaded. Image metadata, including any location data the camera recorded, is stripped before the photo is saved.
  • The QR scanner processes camera frames on the device solely to decode Jerbventory's own printed label identifiers. Frames are not saved, uploaded, used for card recognition, or sent to a catalog or pricing service.
  • Attached photos are not analysed, recognised, priced, matched, or sent to any service.
  • Android supplies attachment photos through the system picker. Android and iOS request camera access when you open the QR scanner; iOS also requests it when you choose to take an attachment photo.

You can revoke camera access at any time in your device settings. Camera-based attachment and label scanning will stop working, but the rest of the app continues to work.

Our pricing service

Jerbventory refreshes card prices through a service we operate, hosted on Render. Versions that offer automatic sign-up reach it at pricing.jerbventory.com; test versions use a separate test service run the same way. It exists so that the pricing provider's credential never has to be inside the app, and so prices keep working offline at a show once they have been fetched.

How access works. Pricing is switched on in one of two ways. Either way, the app generates a random device token and registers it with our service, and the token is sent with every pricing request. On iPhone and iPad it is kept in the system keychain. iOS can keep keychain items after an app is deleted, so in versions that offer automatic sign-up the next installation of Jerbventory deletes any token left behind before it contacts the pricing service, and creates a new one when it next needs prices. On Android it is kept in the app's private storage, which uninstalling deletes. The token is not derived from your phone's hardware identifiers, is not linked to your name or email, and is not used for anything except authorising pricing requests.

  • Automatic sign-up (in versions that offer it; these are meant to be the App Store and Google Play versions). The app signs up quietly the first time it needs prices — when you check for card catalog downloads, open a screen that shows catalog prices, or when the nightly refresh has priced cards to update. Nothing is sent if you never use prices. Before registering the token, the app asks your phone's platform to confirm that the request comes from the genuine Jerbventory app on a genuine device: Apple App Attest on iPhone and iPad, Google Play Integrity on Android. Apple or Google produces that confirmation under its own privacy policy, and the app sends it to our service together with the token. Our service checks it on our own server, without contacting Apple or Google, and then discards it; the confirmation is not stored and not logged. Apple describes App Attest confirmations as anonymous and free of hardware identifiers. Google describes Play Integrity as using information about the app (such as its package name, version and signing certificate), the app's Google Play licence status, and device attestation information from Google Play services, to check that the app and device are genuine. If Apple or Google cannot confirm the app or the device (for example a modified device, or a copy of the Android app that Google does not recognise as the Google Play version), automatic pricing stays off on that device, you can still enter prices yourself, and the app tries again at most once a day. Settings shows whether automatic pricing is on and this device's pricing device identifier.
  • Activation code (testers). A one-time code we send you, which registers the token.

Limiting sign-up abuse. Our service limits how many sign-up attempts can come from one network address in an hour. To do that it turns your IP address into one-way codes (keyed hashes; for IPv6, of the address blocks your address belongs to rather than of the single address), using a random key that changes every day. It keeps those codes only in the service's working memory, never writes them to disk or to a log, and never links them to your device token. Each code is erased when the day ends in UTC, or sooner once it is no longer needed, and always within 24 hours; the day's key is erased with the last code made with it. The IP address itself is not kept at all.

What is sent. When prices are refreshed — each night, when you ask, or when you open a catalog screen that shows prices — the app asks the service for the prices of specific products by sending their catalog identifiers: the products you track and, on a catalog screen, the products shown on it, whether or not you own them. If the service cannot answer for every product you track, the nightly or requested refresh downloads the whole price guide (a table of catalog identifiers and price tiers) instead. Those identifiers tell our service which card products you follow or looked at. They do not include quantities, costs, asking prices, sale records, show data, photos, notes, or anything that identifies you. To protect the service from overload, it counts how many products each device asks about, in its working memory only; the count is never written to disk or to a log and is lost whenever the service restarts. A device that asks for too many in a short time gets the whole price guide on its next nightly refresh instead.

What we keep.

  • For each automatic sign-up: a device identifier, a hashed copy of the device token (we cannot read the token back), whether the phone is iOS or Android, and the date (not the time) it signed up and was last used. On iPhone and iPad we also keep a hashed copy of the App Attest key identifier, that key's public key and a counter, which let us recognise the same app instance later; they say nothing about you. These records are deleted automatically after 12 months without use.
  • For each activation code: the activation code's identifier, the device identifier, a hashed copy of the device token, the dates it was issued, activated, and, if applicable, revoked, and an optional short label we may have typed when creating the activation code, such as a device nickname.
  • A sign-up request that is still in progress holds a random one-time challenge for at most five minutes.

Our hosting provider also keeps ordinary web-server connection records (IP address, time, and the path requested) under its own retention rules, and keeps automatic backups of the service's storage for a limited period, so a deleted record can remain in those backups until they expire. Our own service logs record only fixed outcome codes, never IP addresses, tokens or sign-up confirmations. We do not keep the lists of identifiers you looked up beyond what is needed to answer the request.

Deleting it. On Android, uninstalling the app discards the token. On iPhone and iPad the keychain may keep it until Jerbventory is installed again; in versions that offer automatic sign-up, the app then deletes it before it contacts the pricing service, so it is never used again. To have your activation record removed from our service, or to have a lost device's token revoked, email us at the address below with the device nickname or the approximate activation date; we will revoke it and confirm. An automatic sign-up record holds no name, label or contact detail, so we usually cannot tell which one is yours; in versions that offer automatic sign-up the app shows its pricing device identifier in Settings, so send us that and we will delete the record. Otherwise it is deleted automatically after 12 months without use.

Provider. Prices come from PriceCharting and, for sports cards, SportsCardsPro, under a written arrangement with PriceCharting. Every price the app gets from them is labelled with its source. A card's detail screen has a "View on PriceCharting" link (for sports cards, "View on SportsCardsPro") to that product's page, and screens that show many prices or totals have a linked "Prices from PriceCharting" line. Prices you type or import yourself are never labelled as theirs. Those links open only when you tap them; your browser then contacts that site, and its own privacy policy applies. Our service, not your phone, is what fetches prices from them, and they receive nothing about you or your device from us.

If your device is offline, the app continues to work; price lookups simply become unavailable and are reported as unavailable rather than guessed.

Links that open outside the app

For price research, Jerbventory can open your browser at eBay, TCGplayer, or 130point with a search you initiate, and at PriceCharting and SportsCardsPro (product and home pages) when you tap a price-source link. These are ordinary web links, not integrations. Once your browser opens, that site's own privacy policy applies and we have no visibility into what happens there. Nothing is stored or synced back into the app.

Backups and exports

You can export a backup archive, and you choose where it goes — local storage, Files, iCloud Drive, Google Drive, or anywhere else your device offers.

  • The app's own backups contain your full app data, including financial records and the prices already recorded on your cards. They leave out the cached price guide and downloaded card catalogs. After a restore, the app downloads the price guide again with its next daily price update; downloaded card catalogs are not downloaded again automatically, and you can download them again from Settings.
  • Your phone's own backups (iCloud or a computer on iPhone; Android's backup and device transfer) copy the app's database as it is, including any cached prices; downloaded price-guide and catalog files are left out.
  • We never receive them.
  • If you place a backup in a third-party cloud service, that service's terms and privacy policy govern it from that point.
  • On Android, the app's install identifier is deliberately excluded from system cloud backup and device transfer.

Treat a backup like a copy of your books, because that is what it is.

What we never do

  • Sell, rent, or share your data — we do not have it
  • Show advertisements
  • Track you across apps or websites
  • Use your data to train anything
  • Require an account to use core functionality

Children

Jerbventory is a business tool for people selling collectible cards and is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect information from anyone, of any age.

Deleting your data

Uninstalling the app deletes its local database and photos.

This is permanent. Because we hold no copy, we cannot restore anything for you. Export a backup before uninstalling, switching devices, or resetting a device.

Your rights

Privacy laws such as the GDPR and CCPA give you rights to access, correct, export, and delete personal data a company holds about you. Your inventory and financial data are entirely in your possession — visible in the app, exportable as a backup, and deletable by uninstalling. The only records we hold are the pricing-service records described above, which identify a device token rather than you; contact us and we will help with any request about them.

Changes to this policy

If the app's data handling changes — for example if optional cloud sync, accounts, or crash reporting are ever added — this policy will be updated before that version is released, and the change will be described in the release notes. Material changes will be surfaced in the app.
