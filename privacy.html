Jerbventory Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 3, 2026

Last updated: November 3, 2026

Applies to: Jerbventory for iPhone/iPad and Android

The short version

Jerbventory has no Jerbventory account or advertising analytics. Your inventory, sales, shows, photos, and financial records stay on your device unless you deliberately export them somewhere, or post a listing to eBay from the app.

The app talks to our own pricing service, so that card prices can be refreshed each day. It receives an anonymous device token and the catalog identifiers of cards you track or look at in a catalog, never your records themselves. In versions that sign up for pricing automatically, Apple or Google first confirms to our service that it is the genuine app; we check that confirmation and do not keep it. To stop abuse of that sign-up, the service briefly uses a scrambled form of your network address, which it never stores and forgets within 24 hours. The details are in the section "Our pricing service" below.

Public paid versions also use Apple or Google to offer and restore Vendor subscriptions. Android sends a purchase token to our service for verification with Google; it is processed for that request and is not stored or logged by our service. Google Play Billing includes SDK diagnostics. See "Subscriptions and store services" below.

If you choose to sell on eBay from the app, a second service of ours, the eBay connector, holds an encrypted authorization for your eBay seller account, the photos of listings you post, and the order details it reads from eBay so your sales reach the app. It never keeps buyer names, addresses or contact details. The details are in the section "eBay selling" below.

We never sell personal information or use it for advertising. "Who receives data" below lists every company that handles it.

What we collect

Jerbventory has no Jerbventory accounts or Jerbventory sign-in, no advertising identifiers, and no first-party analytics or crash reporting. (Connecting eBay uses eBay's own sign-in page; see "eBay selling".) Native store services and Google Play Billing process subscription information and SDK diagnostics under their own policies. We do not build a profile of you.

Data that reaches a server we operate includes what the pricing service needs to work, described in "Our pricing service" below: an anonymous device token, the catalog identifiers of cards you track or look at in a catalog, and the ordinary connection records any web server keeps. In versions that offer automatic sign-up, it also receives a one-time confirmation from Apple or Google when the app signs up, and a short-lived scrambled form of your network address used only to limit sign-up attempts. Android subscription verification also sends its purchase token with the enrolled device credential, as described below. If you connect an eBay seller account, the eBay connector described in "eBay selling" below also keeps what it needs to sell for you.

If you email us, we receive your email address and your message, and use them only to answer you.

What stays on your device

All of the following is stored locally, on your device only:

  • Inventory and Collection records, including card details, quantities, condition, and grading
  • Acquisition cost, asking price, market values, and every calculated financial figure
  • Sales, bundles, trades, purchases, fees, expenses, and show records
  • Transaction history, reversals, and closeout results
  • Photos you attach to a Quick Capture, a card, or a show
  • App preferences and settings

On Android this is a local database in the app's private storage. On iOS it is a local database in the app's container. Neither is transmitted to us.

Camera, photos, and label scanning

Jerbventory uses the camera only for local actions you choose: attaching a photo to a transaction or card on iOS, and scanning a Jerbventory QR inventory label on Android or iOS.

  • Photos attached to a Quick Capture, a card or a show are stored on your device only and are never uploaded. Photos you add to an eBay listing are different: they are uploaded when you post that listing (see "eBay selling").
  • A photo attached to a transaction or a card is re-encoded before it is saved, which removes the image metadata, including any location the camera recorded.
  • A photo attached to a show is saved as a copy of the file you picked, with its metadata, including any location the camera recorded. It stays on your device and is included in your phone's own backups; it is never uploaded. If that matters to you, choose a photo without location data or remove it first.
  • The QR scanner processes camera frames on the device solely to decode Jerbventory's own printed label identifiers. Frames are not saved, uploaded, used for card recognition, or sent to a catalog or pricing service.
  • Attached photos are not analysed, recognised, priced, matched, or sent to any service. eBay listing photos are sent only to our eBay connector, so that eBay can show them in your listing.
  • Android supplies attachment photos through the system picker. Android and iOS request camera access when you open the QR scanner; iOS also requests it when you choose to take an attachment photo.

You can revoke camera access at any time in your device settings. Camera-based attachment and label scanning will stop working, but the rest of the app continues to work.

Our pricing service

Jerbventory refreshes card prices through a service we operate, hosted on Render. Versions that offer automatic sign-up reach it at pricing.jerbventory.com; test versions use a separate test service run the same way. It exists so that the pricing provider's credential never has to be inside the app, and so prices keep working offline at a show once they have been fetched.

How access works. Pricing is switched on in one of two ways. Either way, the app generates a random device token and registers it with our service. The token authorises catalog and pricing requests and, on Android, Vendor billing status and purchase verification requests. On iPhone and iPad it is kept in the system keychain. iOS can keep keychain items after an app is deleted, so in versions that offer automatic sign-up the next installation of Jerbventory deletes any token left behind before it contacts the pricing service, and creates a new one when it next needs prices. On Android it is kept in the app's private storage, which uninstalling deletes. The token is not derived from your phone's hardware identifiers, is not linked to your name or email, and is used only to authorise these catalog, pricing and Android Vendor verification requests.

  • Automatic sign-up (in versions that offer it; these are meant to be the App Store and Google Play versions). The app signs up quietly the first time it needs prices — when you check for card catalog downloads, open a screen that shows catalog prices, or when the nightly refresh has priced cards to update. On Android, checking Vendor billing availability or verifying a purchase can also trigger sign-up before prices have been fetched. Before registering the token, the app asks your phone's platform to confirm that the request comes from the genuine Jerbventory app on a genuine device: Apple App Attest on iPhone and iPad, Google Play Integrity on Android. Apple or Google produces that confirmation under its own privacy policy, and the app sends it to our service together with the token. Our service checks it on our own server, without contacting Apple or Google, and then discards it; the confirmation is not stored and not logged. Apple describes App Attest confirmations as anonymous and free of hardware identifiers. Google describes Play Integrity as using information about the app (such as its package name, version and signing certificate), the app's Google Play licence status, and device attestation information from Google Play services, to check that the app and device are genuine. If Apple or Google cannot confirm the app or the device (for example a modified device, or a copy of the Android app that Google does not recognise as the Google Play version), automatic pricing stays off on that device, you can still enter prices yourself, and the app tries again later: at most four confirmation attempts in any 24 hours, with a growing wait between them, and only when prices are needed again. Settings shows whether automatic pricing is on and this device's pricing device identifier.
  • Activation code (testers). A one-time code we send you, which registers the token.

Limiting sign-up abuse. Our service limits how many sign-up attempts can come from one network address in an hour. To do that it turns your IP address into one-way codes (keyed hashes; for IPv6, of the address blocks your address belongs to rather than of the single address), using a random key that changes every day. It keeps those codes only in the service's working memory, never writes them to disk or to a log, and never links them to your device token. Each code is erased when the day ends in UTC, or sooner once it is no longer needed, and always within 24 hours; the day's key is erased with the last code made with it. The IP address itself is not kept at all.

What is sent. When prices are refreshed — each night, when you ask, or when you open a catalog screen that shows prices — the app asks the service for the prices of specific products by sending their catalog identifiers: the products you track and, on a catalog screen, the products shown on it, whether or not you own them. If the service cannot answer for every product you track, the nightly or requested refresh downloads the whole price guide (a table of catalog identifiers and price tiers) instead. Those identifiers tell our service which card products you follow or looked at. They do not include quantities, costs, asking prices, sale records, show data, photos, notes, or anything that identifies you. To protect the service from overload, it counts how many products each device asks about, in its working memory only; the count is never written to disk or to a log and is lost whenever the service restarts. A device that asks for too many in a short time gets the whole price guide on its next nightly refresh instead.

What we keep.

  • For each automatic sign-up: a device identifier, a hashed copy of the device token (we cannot read the token back), whether the phone is iOS or Android, and the date (not the time) it signed up and was last used. On iPhone and iPad we also keep a hashed copy of the App Attest key identifier, that key's public key and a counter, which let us recognise the same app instance later; they say nothing about you. These records are deleted automatically after 12 months without use.
  • For each activation code: the activation code's identifier, the device identifier, a hashed copy of the device token, the dates it was issued, activated, and, if applicable, revoked, and an optional short label we may have typed when creating the activation code, such as a device nickname.
  • A sign-up request that is still in progress holds a random one-time challenge for at most five minutes.

Our hosting provider, Render, also keeps ordinary web-server connection records (IP address, time, and the path requested) for about 7 days, and takes a daily backup of the service's storage that it keeps for 7 days, so a deleted record can remain in those backups for up to 7 days. Our own service logs record only fixed outcome codes, never IP addresses, tokens or sign-up confirmations. We do not keep the lists of identifiers you looked up beyond what is needed to answer the request.

Deleting it. On Android, uninstalling the app discards the token. On iPhone and iPad the keychain may keep it until Jerbventory is installed again; in versions that offer automatic sign-up, the app then deletes it before it contacts the pricing service, so it is never used again. To have your activation record removed from our service, or to have a lost device's token revoked, email us at the address below with the device nickname or the approximate activation date; we will revoke it and confirm. An automatic sign-up record holds no name, label or contact detail, so we usually cannot tell which one is yours; in versions that offer automatic sign-up the app shows its pricing device identifier in Settings, so send us that and we will delete the record. Otherwise it is deleted automatically after 12 months without use.

Provider. Prices come from PriceCharting and, for sports cards, SportsCardsPro, under a written arrangement with PriceCharting. Every price the app gets from them is labelled with its source. A card's detail screen has a "View on PriceCharting" link (for sports cards, "View on SportsCardsPro") to that product's page, and screens that show many prices or totals have a linked "Prices from PriceCharting" line. Prices you type or import yourself are never labelled as theirs. Those links open only when you tap them; your browser then contacts that site, and its own privacy policy applies. Our service, not your phone, is what fetches prices from them, and they receive nothing about you or your device from us.

Subscriptions and store services

Public versions offer Free access and optional monthly or annual Vendor subscriptions through Apple's App Store or Google Play. Your platform store handles product information, checkout, payment, renewal and restoration using your store account. Jerbventory does not receive your payment-card information. A subscription restores within its original platform/store account; there is no Jerbventory account linking Apple and Google purchases.

On iPhone and iPad, the app accepts cryptographically verified StoreKit transactions and renewal information. It saves a compact entitlement summary and the consumed Show-trial identity in device-only Keychain storage, outside workspace backups. It does not send Apple transaction proof to a Jerbventory server. iOS may keep these device-only items after app deletion; uninstalling does not cancel the store subscription.

On Android, the app sends the Google purchase token to our service at /v1/billing/google/verify, authenticated with the existing pricing device credential. Our service asks Google's Play Developer API to verify the subscription and acknowledges eligible new purchases. Purchase tokens and Google response bodies are processed for the request only; our service does not store or log them. The server's Google OAuth access token is memory-only. The app saves encrypted purchase proof/token and the Show-trial marker in private no-backup storage for offline access. Restoring a workspace backup does not grant a subscription or a new Show trial.

Google Play Billing includes Google's own reporting components, which may send diagnostic information to Google under Google's privacy policy. They have no access to your Inventory, photos, financial records, or location. Apple and Google handle their store services under their own privacy policies.

For price research, Jerbventory can open your browser at eBay or 130point with a search you initiate, and at PriceCharting and SportsCardsPro (product and home pages) when you tap a price-source link. These are ordinary web links, not integrations. Once your browser opens, that site's own privacy policy applies and we have no visibility into what happens there. Nothing is stored or synced back into the app.

eBay selling (optional, Vendor plan)

Selling on eBay from the app uses a second service we operate, the eBay connector, hosted on Render and reached at marketplace.jerbventory.com. eBay's own credentials for Jerbventory live only on that service, never in the app.

Connecting. Connecting opens eBay's own sign-in page in your browser; Jerbventory never sees your eBay password. When the connector asks for it, the app first gets the same genuine-app confirmation described in "Our pricing service" (Apple App Attest or Google Play Integrity). The connector checks it and discards it, keeping only which kind of check this device passed and when. A sign-in in progress uses a random one-time value that expires after ten minutes, and a genuine-app check uses a random one-time challenge that expires after five minutes; expired ones are deleted the next time any sign-in starts.

What the connector keeps.

  • Your eBay seller account: its eBay username, eBay's permanent user ID for it, and an authorization from eBay (access and refresh tokens) that lets the connector act on that account. The authorization is encrypted with a key held only by the service.
  • Your phones: for each phone connected to the account, a hashed copy of the app's random connector token (we cannot read the token back), when it was first and last seen, and whether it passed the genuine-app check. The token is not derived from your phone's hardware identifiers.
  • Listings you post from the app: the identifiers that tie each listing to eBay (Jerbventory's listing ID, the SKU, eBay's offer and listing numbers), the random identifier of the Inventory lot, and its state and quantity. The title, description, price and item details you write are sent to eBay to create the listing; the connector does not keep its own copy. If you ask, it also turns on eBay business policies for your account, creates standard shipping and return policies, and creates your ship-from location on eBay from a ZIP code you enter. The ZIP code is passed to eBay and not kept by the connector.
  • Listing photos: the photos you choose for a listing are uploaded to the connector when you post it and published at a web address eBay uses to show them. Anyone who has that address can view the photo, and eBay, browsers and other services may keep their own copies. The connector deletes a listing's photos 30 days after the last listing using them ends, photos uploaded for a listing that is never posted 30 days after upload, and all of them when your eBay data is deleted (below).
  • Orders: the connector reads your eBay orders regularly, including while the app is closed: about every 5 minutes while you have a live listing, and less often otherwise. It does this so a sale reaches your History even if your phone was off. For each order line on the connected account changed since you connected, including orders for listings you did not post from Jerbventory, it keeps:
  • the order, line and item numbers;
  • the quantity and the sale amount;
  • the payment, shipping and cancellation status;
  • the dates.

It keeps these while the account stays connected, so a sale is never recorded twice. Buyer names, addresses, emails and phone numbers are not kept.

  • Actions you take: when you mark an order shipped or cancel it from the app, the connector sends eBay the carrier and tracking number, or the cancellation reason.

The connector uses this only to publish and update your listings, to ship or cancel orders when you ask, and to bring your sales into your own History. It is not sold, rented, or used for advertising. It is sent to eBay only as eBay needs it to do what you ask, and is otherwise handled only by our hosting provider (see "Who receives data").

Disconnecting and deletion.

  • Disconnect eBay in the app removes this phone's connection. If no other phone is connected and you have no live listings posted from Jerbventory, the connector deletes the account's eBay authorization, listing records, photos and order records at the same time. If listings are still live, it keeps reading their sales, so nothing is missed, until you end them and disconnect, or ask us to delete everything.
  • In versions that offer it, Delete my eBay data now (next to Disconnect eBay) deletes all of the above for your account immediately, even while listings are live. Sales of those listings then stop reaching the app; manage them on eBay.
  • You can also remove Jerbventory's access in your eBay account settings. The connector then stops reading your account, but that alone does not delete what it already holds: use Disconnect eBay, Delete my eBay data now, or email us.
  • If you close your eBay account, eBay notifies the connector and it deletes everything above for that account.
  • To have it deleted sooner, email us at the address below with your eBay username.
  • As with the pricing service, our hosting provider's daily backups can keep a deleted record for up to 7 days.

Who receives data

We share data only with:

  • Render (Render Services, Inc., United States), which hosts our pricing service and eBay connector on servers in Oregon. It stores and processes this data only to provide that hosting to us, under its terms of service and Data Processing Addendum.
  • eBay, when you connect an eBay seller account and list, ship or cancel through the app. eBay handles that data under its own privacy notice.
  • Apple and Google, for the genuine-app checks and the subscriptions described above, under their own privacy policies.

The price providers (PriceCharting and SportsCardsPro) receive nothing about you from us. We never sell or rent personal information, and never share it for advertising.

How we protect data

All traffic between the app and our services uses HTTPS. Device tokens are stored on our servers only as one-way hashes, so we cannot read them back. eBay authorizations are encrypted with a key held only by our eBay connector. Our own service logs record outcome codes, never tokens or confirmations.

Tracking and Do Not Track

Jerbventory does not track you across apps or websites. Apart from Apple's and Google's own store and platform services described above, no other company collects information through Jerbventory about your activity over time or across other websites or apps. Because we do not track you, we do not change anything in response to a browser's Do Not Track signal.

Backups and exports

You can export a backup archive, and you choose where it goes — local storage, Files, iCloud Drive, Google Drive, or anywhere else your device offers.

  • The app's own backups contain your full app data, including financial records and the prices already recorded on your cards. They leave out the cached price guide and downloaded card catalogs. After a restore, the app downloads the price guide again with its next daily price update; downloaded card catalogs are not downloaded again automatically, and you can download them again from Settings.
  • Your phone's own backups (iCloud or a computer on iPhone; Android's backup and device transfer) copy the app's database as it is, including any cached prices; downloaded price-guide and catalog files are left out.
  • We never receive them.
  • If you place a backup in a third-party cloud service, that service's terms and privacy policy govern it from that point.
  • On Android, the app's install identifier is deliberately excluded from system cloud backup and device transfer.

Treat a backup like a copy of your books, because that is what it is.

What we never do

  • Sell or rent your data. Your ledger never reaches us, and what our eBay connector holds goes only to eBay and our hosting provider, as described in "Who receives data"
  • Show advertisements
  • Track you across apps or websites
  • Use your data to train anything
  • Require an account to use core functionality

Children

Jerbventory is a business tool for adults who sell collectible cards. It is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child has given us information, email us and we will delete it.

Your rights

Your inventory and financial data are entirely in your possession: visible in the app, exportable as a backup, and deletable by uninstalling. We hold two kinds of records: the pricing-service records described above, which identify a device token rather than you, and, if you connect eBay, the eBay connector records described in "eBay selling", which are tied to your eBay seller account.

Whatever law applies to you, you can ask us to tell you what we hold, correct it, or delete it. Email jafana@jerbventory.com with your eBay username or the pricing device identifier shown in Settings; we reply within 30 days. We do not sell or share personal information, or use it for advertising.
