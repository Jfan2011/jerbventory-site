Jerbventory Privacy Policy

Effective date: August 10, 2026

Last updated: October 1, 2026

Applies to: Jerbventory for iPhone/iPad and Android

The short version

Jerbventory does not collect your personal data. There are no accounts and no analytics. Your inventory, sales, shows, photos, and financial records stay on your device unless you deliberately export them somewhere.

The one thing the app does talk to is our own pricing service, so that card prices can be refreshed each day. It receives an anonymous device token and the catalog identifiers of cards you track, never your records themselves. When the app first signs up for pricing, Apple or Google confirms to our service that it is the genuine app; we check that confirmation and do not keep it. To stop abuse, the service briefly uses a scrambled form of your network address, which it never stores and forgets within 24 hours. The details are in the section "Our pricing service" below.

Who we are

Jerbventory is published by Jeffrey George Afana. Contact: jafana@jerbventory.com.

What we collect

Jerbventory has no user accounts, no sign-in, no telemetry, no advertising identifiers, no crash reporting, and no analytics SDK of any kind. We do not build a profile of you.

The only data that reaches a server we operate is what the pricing service needs to work, described in "Our pricing service" below: an anonymous device token, the catalog identifiers of cards you track, a one-time confirmation from Apple or Google when the app signs up, a short-lived scrambled form of your network address used only to limit sign-up attempts, and the ordinary connection records any web server keeps.

What stays on your device

All of the following is stored locally, on your device only:

  • Inventory and Collection records, including card details, quantities, condition, and grading
  • Acquisition cost, asking price, market values, and every calculated financial figure
  • Sales, bundles, trades, purchases, fees, expenses, and show records
  • Transaction history, reversals, and closeout results
  • Photos you attach to a Quick Capture or a card
  • App preferences and settings

On Android this is a local database in the app's private storage. On iOS it is a local database in the app's container. Neither is transmitted to us.

Camera, photos, and label scanning

Jerbventory uses the camera only for local actions you choose: attaching a photo to a transaction or card on iOS, and scanning a Jerbventory QR inventory label on Android or iOS.

  • Attached photos are stored on your device only and are never uploaded. Image metadata, including any location data the camera recorded, is stripped before the photo is saved.
  • The QR scanner processes camera frames on the device solely to decode Jerbventory's own printed label identifiers. Frames are not saved, uploaded, used for card recognition, or sent to a catalog or pricing service.
  • Attached photos are not analysed, recognised, priced, matched, or sent to any service.
  • Android supplies attachment photos through the system picker. Android and iOS request camera access when you open the QR scanner; iOS also requests it when you choose to take an attachment photo.

You can revoke camera access at any time in your device settings. Camera-based attachment and label scanning will stop working, but the rest of the app continues to work.

Our pricing service

Jerbventory refreshes card prices through a service we operate, hosted on Render. It exists so that the pricing provider's credential never has to be inside the app, and so prices keep working offline at a show once they have been fetched.

How access works. Pricing is switched on in one of two ways. Either way, the app generates a random device token and registers it with our service; the token is stored in the app's private storage (in the system keychain on iOS) and is sent with every pricing request. The token is not derived from your phone's hardware identifiers, is not linked to your name or email, and is not used for anything except authorising pricing requests.

  • Automatic sign-up (versions that offer it). Before registering the token, the app asks your phone's platform to confirm that the request comes from the genuine Jerbventory app on a genuine device: Apple App Attest on iPhone and iPad, Google Play Integrity on Android. Apple or Google produces that confirmation under its own privacy policy, and the app sends it to our service together with the token. Our service checks it on our own server, without contacting Apple or Google, and then discards it; the confirmation is not stored and not logged.
  • Activation code (testers). A one-time code we send you, which registers the token.

Limiting sign-up abuse. Our service limits how many sign-up attempts can come from one network address in an hour. To do that it turns your IP address into one-way codes (keyed hashes; for IPv6, of the address blocks your address belongs to rather than of the single address), using a random key that changes every day. It keeps those codes only in the service's working memory, never writes them to disk or to a log, and never links them to your device token. Each code is erased when the day ends in UTC, or sooner once it is no longer needed, and always within 24 hours; the day's key is erased with the last code made with it. The IP address itself is not kept at all.

What is sent. Each day, or when you ask for a refresh, the app downloads a price guide (a table of catalog identifiers and price tiers) and, for cards the guide does not cover, asks the service for prices by sending the catalog identifiers of cards you track. Those identifiers tell our service which card products you follow. They do not include quantities, costs, asking prices, sale records, show data, photos, notes, or anything that identifies you.

What we keep.

  • For each automatic sign-up: a device identifier, a hashed copy of the device token (we cannot read the token back), whether the phone is iOS or Android, and the date (not the time) it signed up and was last used. On iPhone and iPad we also keep a hashed copy of the App Attest key identifier, that key's public key and a counter, which let us recognise the same app instance later; they say nothing about you. These records are deleted automatically after 12 months without use.
  • For each activation code: the activation code's identifier, the device identifier, a hashed copy of the device token, the dates it was issued, activated, and, if applicable, revoked, and an optional short label we may have typed when creating the activation code, such as a device nickname.
  • A sign-up request that is still in progress holds a random one-time challenge for at most five minutes.

Our hosting provider also keeps ordinary web-server connection records (IP address, time, and the path requested) under its own retention rules, and keeps automatic backups of the service's storage for a limited period, so a deleted record can remain in those backups until they expire. Our own service logs record only fixed outcome codes, never IP addresses, tokens or sign-up confirmations. We do not keep the lists of identifiers you looked up beyond what is needed to answer the request.

Deleting it. Uninstalling the app discards the token. To have your activation record removed from our service, or to have a lost device's token revoked, email us at the address below with the device nickname or the approximate activation date; we will revoke it and confirm. An automatic sign-up record holds no name, label or contact detail, so we usually cannot tell which one is yours; if the app shows you its pricing device identifier, send us that and we will delete the record. Otherwise it is deleted automatically after 12 months without use.

Provider. Prices come from PriceCharting and SportsCardsPro under a written arrangement with them, and each price in the app links to the product page it came from. Our service, not your phone, is what talks to PriceCharting; they do not receive anything about you or your device.

They do not include your name, your inventory, your costs, your prices, your sales, or any account identifier — because the app has none. If your device is offline, the app continues to work; price lookups simply become unavailable and are reported as unavailable rather than guessed.

Links that open outside the app

For price research, Jerbventory can open your browser at eBay, TCGplayer, or 130point with a search you initiate. This is an ordinary web link, not an integration. Once your browser opens, that site's own privacy policy applies and we have no visibility into what happens there. Nothing is stored or synced back into the app.

Backups and exports

You can export a backup archive, and you choose where it goes — local storage, Files, iCloud Drive, Google Drive, or anywhere else your device offers.

  • Backups contain your full app data, including financial records.
  • We never receive them.
  • If you place a backup in a third-party cloud service, that service's terms and privacy policy govern it from that point.
  • On Android, the app's install identifier is deliberately excluded from system cloud backup and device transfer.

Treat a backup like a copy of your books, because that is what it is.

What we never do

  • Sell, rent, or share your data — we do not have it
  • Show advertisements
  • Track you across apps or websites
  • Use your data to train anything
  • Require an account to use core functionality

Children

Jerbventory is a business tool for people selling collectible cards and is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect information from anyone, of any age.

Deleting your data

Uninstalling the app deletes its local database and photos.

This is permanent. Because we hold no copy, we cannot restore anything for you. Export a backup before uninstalling, switching devices, or resetting a device.

Your rights

Privacy laws such as the GDPR and CCPA give you rights to access, correct, export, and delete personal data a company holds about you. Your inventory and financial data are entirely in your possession — visible in the app, exportable as a backup, and deletable by uninstalling. The only records we hold are the pricing-service records described above, which identify a device token rather than you; contact us and we will help with any request about them.

Changes to this policy

If the app's data handling changes — for example if optional cloud sync, accounts, or crash reporting are ever added — this policy will be updated before that version is released, and the change will be described in the release notes. Material changes will be surfaced in the app.
